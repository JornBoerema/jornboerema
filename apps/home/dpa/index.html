Data processing agreement

In Dutch: verwerkersovereenkomst. This agreement applies when a business or other organisation uses the Even apps for its people, and so has Even process personal data on its behalf, as Article 28 of the GDPR requires. Last updated 15 November 2026.

1. The parties

The customer is the organisation that uses Even for its work: it takes a subscription for an organisation in Even, or lets its people use Even for work. The customer decides why personal data is processed and is the controller.

The processor is Jorn Boerema, a sole proprietorship at Snellingerdijk 30, 8431 EN Oosterwolde, the Netherlands, Chamber of Commerce 42064465, VAT NL005468091B10, which makes and runs Even.

The customer accepts this agreement by using Even for its organisation. It is part of the terms of use. If you want a signed copy, mail support@jornboerema.nl and you receive one with the same text.

2. What is processed

Purpose: providing the Even apps (mail, to-do, notes and what is added later) to the customer's people, and nothing else.

Whose data: the customer's members and the people they mail with or write about.

What data:

  • account details: name, email address, sign-in details and organisation membership;
  • for connected mailboxes: the address, encrypted access tokens and the message details described in the privacy policy (sender, recipients, subject, a short preview, date, folder, read status, and summaries and details the app worked out); message bodies and attachments are fetched from Google or Microsoft when opened and not stored;
  • to-do items, boards, notes and whatever else the members put in them;
  • billing details of the organisation, handled by Stripe.

The customer makes sure no special categories of personal data (such as health data) are put into the apps unless that is lawful, and that it may let its people connect the mailboxes they connect.

3. Only on the customer's instructions

The processor processes the data only to provide Even as described in the terms, the privacy policy and the settings the customer's people choose, which together are the customer's instructions. It does not use the data for its own purposes: not for advertising, not for selling, and not for training AI models. If the law requires something else, the processor tells the customer first, unless the law forbids that. If an instruction breaks the GDPR, the processor says so.

4. Confidentiality

Only the processor and anyone working for it who needs to may see the data, and only to keep the service running, fix a problem a member reports, or comply with the law. All of them are bound to secrecy.

5. Security

The processor keeps the data safe with at least these measures:

  • all connections over HTTPS; the database and backups encrypted at rest;
  • access tokens to mailboxes encrypted separately and never sent to the browser;
  • data kept in the European Union (Frankfurt), apart from the subprocessors marked otherwise below;
  • each member sees only their own mail and the data of organisations they belong to;
  • admin access to hosting and database limited to the processor;
  • mail shown in a sealed frame that can't run scripts, outside pictures blocked until allowed;
  • error reports that say where something went wrong, without mail contents or who it was;
  • a status page and alerts when part of the service stops working.

6. Subprocessors

The customer allows the processor to use the subprocessors in the list below. Each is bound by a data processing agreement with at least the same protection as this one. A new or replaced subprocessor is announced by email to the organisation's owners at least 30 days ahead; the customer can object with good reason, and if no solution is found it can end its subscription before the change applies, with a refund of what it paid for the time after that.

7. Outside the European Union

Some subprocessors are in the United States. Transfers to them rely on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework where the company is certified under it, and otherwise on the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, with the extra measures described here.

8. Helping the customer

The processor helps the customer answer requests from people who want to see, correct, export or delete their data, and with data protection impact assessments and questions from a supervisory authority, as far as it reasonably can. Most of this the members can do themselves in the app: disconnecting a mailbox or deleting an account removes the data straight away. A request that reaches the processor directly is passed on to the customer.

9. Data breaches

If the processor finds a breach of personal data, it tells the organisation's owners without undue delay and at the latest within 48 hours, with what is known: what happened, which data and people are affected, the likely consequences and what is being done about it. It keeps the customer informed after that, so the customer can report it to the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens within 72 hours where needed. The customer makes that report; the processor does not do it in the customer's name.

10. Checking

On request the processor gives the customer the information needed to show it keeps to this agreement. If that is not enough, the customer can have an independent auditor check it, at the customer's cost, at most once a year, announced at least 30 days ahead and under a duty of secrecy.

11. When it ends

This agreement runs as long as the processor processes data for the customer. When the customer stops, its members can export or delete their data first; within 30 days after the organisation or account is deleted, the processor removes the data, also from subprocessors, unless the law requires keeping it (such as invoices, for the tax office). Backups age out within 30 days after that.

12. Liability and law

Liability follows the terms of use. If this agreement and the terms disagree on the protection of personal data, this agreement wins. Dutch law applies; disputes go to the court in the Netherlands that the law points to.

Annex: subprocessors

  • Vercel (servers in Frankfurt; company in the United States): runs the app and the API.
  • Neon (Frankfurt; company in the United States): the database, and the signal that tells an open app something changed.
  • Google and Microsoft: the connected mailboxes themselves, which stay with them under the customer's own agreement with them.
  • OpenRouter and Anthropic (United States): the AI features, only for the conversation a member is looking at, without keeping it or training on it; each feature can be turned off.
  • Trigger.dev: the timer for snoozed mail and mail sent later. It sees no mail.
  • Resend: emails about accounts, invitations and alerts.
  • Stripe: payments and invoices for the subscription.
  • Sentry (Germany): error reports, without mail contents or who it was.
  • Apple: notifications on iPhone and iPad, when a member turns them on.
  • Moneybird: only the invoices a member chooses to send there, when they connect it themselves.
