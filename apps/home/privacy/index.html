Privacy policy

This policy covers Even mailen, an email app that shows the Gmail and Outlook mailboxes you connect to it. Last updated 13 November 2026.

Who runs it

Even mailen is made by Jorn Boerema in the Netherlands, who is responsible for your data under the GDPR. Questions and requests can be sent through GitHub.

What the app accesses

When you connect a mailbox, you give the app permission through Google or Microsoft to read, label, move and send email in that mailbox. It also receives your name and email address, to show which mailbox is connected. The app only acts on your mail when you use it.

What is stored

  • Your Evido account: name, email address and sign-in details.
  • For each connected mailbox: its address, your name and encrypted access tokens.
  • Message details needed for your mail lists, up to a year back: sender, recipients, subject, a short preview, date, folder and read status.
  • What the app worked out for you, so it doesn't have to read the same mail twice: a short summary and reply suggestions per conversation, a one-time code, and for parcels and invoices the details shown in their lists (such as carrier, tracking code, amount and due date).
  • What you set up yourself: signatures, settings, pinned and snoozed conversations, and the subject and recipients of mail scheduled to send later.
  • Attachments you are adding to a message, until it is sent or two days pass.
  • If you turn on notifications on a phone or tablet: the address Apple gives that device for notifications.
  • How much the AI features were used on your account each month, without any mail.

Message bodies and attachments from your mailbox are not stored. They are fetched from Google or Microsoft when you open a message.

How your data is used

Your data is only used to show you your own mail and carry out what you ask the app to do. It is not sold, not used for advertising, and not used to train AI models. Nobody reads your mail, unless you ask for help with a problem or the law requires it.

Even mailen's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

AI features

Summaries, reply suggestions, translation and help with writing are made by a language model. For these, the text of the conversation you are looking at is sent to the model and the answer comes back; it is sent through OpenRouter to Anthropic's Claude, only to providers that agreed not to keep what they receive and not to train on it. One-time codes, parcels and invoices are recognised by the app itself, without a model.

Summaries, reply suggestions and writing help can each be turned off in the app's privacy settings; translation only runs when you ask for it. Turned off, nothing from your mail is sent to a model.

Who processes it

These companies process data for the app, each only for its own part:

  • Google and Microsoft: your mailbox itself, which stays with them.
  • Vercel: runs the app, in Frankfurt.
  • Neon: the database, and the signal that tells an open app something changed.
  • OpenRouter and Anthropic (United States): the AI features described above.
  • Trigger.dev: the timer for snoozed mail and mail sent later. It sees no mail.
  • Resend: emails about your account.
  • Apple: notifications on iPhone and iPad, if you turn them on.
  • Moneybird: only if you connect it yourself, the invoices you choose to send there.

Your data is not shared with anyone else.

Security

Access tokens are encrypted at rest and never sent to your browser. All connections use HTTPS. Mail is shown in a sealed frame that can't run scripts, and pictures from outside are not loaded until you allow them, so a sender can't see that you opened their mail.

Deleting your data

Disconnecting a mailbox removes its tokens and stored details from the app straight away. Deleting your account removes everything linked to it. You can also withdraw access at any time in your Google account or Microsoft account.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct, export or delete your data, and you can complain to the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.
